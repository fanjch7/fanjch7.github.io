Job Market Paper

Designing Leadership under Social Conformity

Abstract

This paper studies the optimal design of leading by example in a social network where players differ in individual productivity and incur a cost for deviating from the average effort of their neighbors (the social norm). Moving first exploits a norm feedback effect: by inducing followers to adjust, a leader reshapes her own social norm and eases peer pressure, which raises aggregate effort if and only if her productivity exceeds that norm. We construct an exact leading index to identify the single key leader and show that as conformity strengthens, selection shifts from local productivity advantages weighted by local reciprocity to a global comparison favoring the most productive player. With multiple leaders, an optimal group requires the remaining followers to form a dominating set; group selection reduces to an NP-hard weighted maximum directed cut problem under weak conformity, whereas under strong conformity a “less is more” principle collapses every optimal group to a single player with the highest productivity. Moreover, finer multi-stage hierarchies need not increase aggregate effort, and when leaders signal private information about an unknown state, retaining a productive player as a follower can outperform appointing her to lead.
