Job Market Paper

Designing Leadership under Social Conformity

Abstract

This paper studies the optimal design of leadership in a local-average network game, in which players differ in individual productivity and incur a conformity cost for deviating from their neighbors’ average effort—the social norm. An organizational planner divides players into leaders and followers across two stages to maximize aggregate effort. We first show that despite the presence of strategic complementarities, leadership can either increase or decrease equilibrium effort relative to simultaneous play, depending on whether a leader’s productivity exceeds the social norm. Next, we derive an exact index to identify the optimal single leader and demonstrate that the selected player can differ substantially from the key leader in the local-aggregate model. We then characterize the limiting structure of optimal leader groups as the taste for conformity approaches zero or infinity. An optimal group favors locally productive players under weak conformity, but collapses to the most productive player under strong conformity. We also analyze multi-stage hierarchies and establish that finer sequencing can be suboptimal. Finally, we extend the model to incorporate incomplete information, anti-conformity, and more general preferences and interactions.
